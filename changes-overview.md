# Changes: multi-asset support

Overview of two connected pieces of work, written for a ziplime maintainer who has to review,
merge and support them.

* **Futures** — the contract chain, continuous futures, rolls, margin, settlement type, and the
  Finam/CME connectors. Detail: [`changes.md`](changes.md).
* **Bonds and cross-asset** — bond pricing and schedules, the bond connector, and equities, bonds
  and futures sharing one portfolio. Detail: [`changes-bonds.md`](changes-bonds.md).

This document is the map: what changed at the level of the whole system, what a maintainer has to
do to adopt it, and what to distrust. The two detail documents keep the evidence for each claim.

**Test suite: 346 tests — 342 passed, 4 xfailed.** The four xfails pre-date this work and are
unchanged. By area: 130 futures, 179 bonds, 16 cross-asset, 12 connector registry, 9 pre-existing
dividend tests.

---

## 1. The short version

Before this work ziplime traded equities. The futures tables existed but nothing wrote to them and
the continuous-future layer was dead code; there was no notion of a bond at all.

It now trades **equities, bonds and futures, together, in one portfolio**, because the engine
dispatches on instrument type wherever the three disagree. That is the through-line of both
workstreams and section 4 is the part worth reading closely.

Along the way **14 bugs** were found and fixed, plus five smaller ones. Six of them affect
equity-only backtests too — most importantly, commission never reached any asset's cost basis,
`order_value` raised for every asset class, and the models passed to `run_simulation` were ignored.

## 2. Read this first: results that moved

Existing backtests will not reproduce their old numbers. Each of these is a fix, not a regression,
but a maintainer comparing against a stored result needs to know which.

| Change | Effect on results | Detail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Variation margin was applied in one direction only | **Large.** A long futures position could not lose money. One Si example went +10.02% → −4.16% | [`changes.md`](changes.md) §1.1 |
| Deliverable contracts force-close before the notice date | Examples trading MOEX single-stock futures roll earlier: +20.10% → +27.39%, −23.01% → −17.79% | [`changes.md`](changes.md) §2.5 |
| `auto_close_date` now sits strictly after the last tradable session | −2.97% → −3.42% on the Si example | [`changes.md`](changes.md) §2.4 |
| Default roll moved one session before auto close | Rolls now book commission and slippage. Rolling *on* auto close let `_cleanup_expired_assets` close the leg for free — the symptom was a calendar-roll strategy booking 22 trades against a volume-roll strategy's 43 | [`changes.md`](changes.md) §2.3 |
| Commission now reaches the cost basis | Reported cost basis and realised P&L move **for equities too**. Cash was always right | [`changes.md`](changes.md) §1.2 |
| Expired positions are now liquidated | Exposure, leverage and position counts change; **P&L does not** — a cash-settled contract closes at its last mark | [`changes-bonds.md`](changes-bonds.md) §7 |
| `perf["positions"]` holds snapshots, not live objects | The recorded past stops mutating as the run proceeds. Cash and P&L unaffected | [`changes.md`](changes.md) §1.7 |
| Bundle load window includes the first session east of UTC | MOEX backtests gain one session at the start | [`changes.md`](changes.md) §1.8 |

## 3. Upgrading

### 3.1 Migrations

The chain is linear; `alembic upgrade head` runs all three new revisions.

```
b8e8c570ad43  (pre-existing)
  └─ b60ffbf8f072   futures_root_symbols + futures_contracts.root_symbol
       └─ c1a4f7d2e9b3   settlement_type + margin_currency on both futures tables
            └─ e5c8b13a7f42   bonds + bond_events        <- shipped DB is here
```

All three are hand-written rather than autogenerated: SQLite has no `ALTER TABLE ADD CONSTRAINT`
and refuses `ADD COLUMN NOT NULL` without a default, so they go through `batch_alter_table`.
Downgrades are implemented and tested.

### 3.2 Dependency

`h2`, via `httpx[http2]`. Not optional for the Finam connector — see [`changes.md`](changes.md)
§3.2 for why HTTP/2 is a correctness requirement rather than a performance choice.

### 3.3 Breaking API changes

| What | Was | Now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ordering a `ContinuousFuture` | silently did something undefined | raises; order the contract `data.current_contract(cf)` returns |
| `MarketImpactBase.get_txn_volume` | `(data, order)` | `(volume, order)` — the old one referenced an undefined name and could never run |
| `AssetService.get_exchange_assets_by_symbols` | one `AssetType` | accepts a sequence, and **raises `AmbiguousSymbol`** when a ticker resolves under more than one |
| `ziplime.data.data_sources.yahoo_finance_*` | module paths | moved under `yahoo/`; old paths are shims that emit `DeprecationWarning` |

### 3.4 Additive signature changes

Nothing below is required; all default to previous behaviour.

* `run_simulation` / `run_simulation_iter`: `bond_commission`, `bond_slippage`,
  `futures_margin_model`, `print_algo`.
* `ingest_market_data`: `assets=` (resolved listings — **preferred**, and effectively required for
  a bundle spanning asset classes), and `asset_type` now accepts a sequence.
* `load_bundle`: `asset_service`, `roll_finder_settings`.
* `SimulationExchange`: `bond_slippage`, `bond_commission`.
* `Ledger`, `PositionTracker`: `bond_book`.
* `calculate_position_tracker_stats`: `bond_book`.
* `set_slippage`, `set_commission`: `bonds=`.

## 4. The asset-class model

This is the architectural change both workstreams converge on, and the thing to understand before
reviewing either.

**Three instrument types settle by rules that contradict each other, and they share one cash
balance, one ledger and one set of performance figures.**

| | cash paid on opening | position value | exposure | pays out via |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Equity** | `price × amount` | `price × amount` | same as value | dividends |
| **Bond** | `dirty price × amount` | dirty value | same as value | coupons, amortization, redemption |
| **Futures** | **nothing** — margin only | **zero** | `price × multiplier × qty` | daily variation margin |

Getting one wrong is not a rounding error. A bond quote is a percentage of face value, so reading
it as money understates the position by a factor of ten on a standard 1000-unit nominal. A future
costs nothing to open, so sizing it by "cash available" is unbounded.

### 4.1 Where the dispatch lives

An instrument is reached as `position.asset.asset` — `Position.asset` and `Transaction.asset` are
the **listing** (`ExchangeAsset`), and `.asset` is the instrument. Getting this wrong was
[`changes.md`](changes.md) §1.3, where every futures branch tested the listing and was therefore
always false.

| Concern | Where |
| --- | --- |
| Cash on a fill | `Ledger.process_transaction` — three branches |
| Position value and exposure | `finance_ext.calculate_position_tracker_stats` |
| Order sizing by money | `TradingAlgorithm._calculate_order_value_amount` |
| Commission and slippage model selection | `SimulationExchange._model_for`, keyed by `type(asset.asset)` |
| Commission into the cost basis | `PositionTracker.adjust_commission_cost_basis` |
| Scheduled payouts | `Ledger.process_dividends`, `process_bond_events`, `redeem_matured_bonds` |

Adding a fourth class means touching that list. `SimulationExchange._model_for` now raises a named
error rather than a bare `KeyError`, so the first thing a new class hits tells you what is missing.

### 4.2 Bond-specific machinery

`ziplime/finance/bonds.py` holds the arithmetic and `BondBook`, which caches coupon and
amortization schedules. It exists because **the ledger settles a transaction inside a synchronous
call** and cannot reach the database there — schedules are loaded when a bond is first ordered and
once per session for every bond held.

Three corrections stand between a bond quote and the ledger: the quotation (percent of face),
accrued interest (the buyer owes the seller), and amortization (the nominal shrinks). See
[`changes-bonds.md`](changes-bonds.md) §1.

### 4.3 Futures-specific machinery

Chains (`futures_root_symbols`), `OrderedContracts`, `RollFinder` / `CalendarRollFinder` /
`VolumeRollFinder`, and continuous-future resolution inside `DataBundle.get_data_by_limit` — rolls
become segments, each read from the contract actually held, back-adjusted onto the newest level.

Plus margin (`ziplime/finance/margin.py`), where the key fact is that **margin currency is not
quote currency**: MOEX collects roubles even for the contracts it quotes in dollars.

### 4.4 One bundle, not three

A simulation reads from a **single** market data source, so every instrument a strategy touches has
to be in one bundle. `ingest_market_data` therefore accepts several asset types — and, better, the
resolved listings, which removes symbol resolution from the path entirely.

That matters more than it sounds: **a ticker is unique only within an asset class.** The shipped
database has 168 tickers that exist under two — `SiH5@RTSX` is a futures contract *and* an equity,
because an equity vendor listed it that way. Resolving by name alone tagged futures bars with the
equity's sid, and the strategy then silently traded nothing.

## 7. Data connectors

`ziplime/data/data_sources/registry.py`. Connectors register themselves and are resolved by name;
nothing in the core imports one, and `ConnectorIndependenceTests` asserts it. Verified by deleting
`ziplime/data/data_sources/finam/` outright: every module still imports and the Yahoo ingest still
runs.

**Finam** covers MOEX FORTS futures, the NYMEX contracts Finam mirrors, MOEX bonds and MOEX
equities. The API has a number of traps that cost real debugging time and are documented where they
bite:

* HTTP/2 is mandatory ([`changes.md`](changes.md) §3.2)
* `Bars` intermittently returns empty for a request that has data — retried
* `contract_size` is **not** a price multiplier
* the `AllAssets` type for a bond is `BONDS`, not `BOND`
* bond redemption arrives as a terminal `AMORTIZATION`, never as `MATURITY`
* `new_face_value` and `coupon_details.face_value` are **today's** outstanding nominal
* `lot_size` is the trading lot and inverts the nominal if mistaken for it
* `limit` is required, `pagination.has_next` is never set, `sort_direction` is ignored, and the
  default date window is about a year

The full table with the consequence of each is [`changes-bonds.md`](changes-bonds.md) §4. All are
covered by tests against **recorded live payloads** (`tests/test_finam_bonds_recorded.py`), so a
regression fails locally without a token.

## 10. Known gaps

Ordered by how likely they are to matter.

* **Cash has no currency.** Margin requirements are reported per currency, but the portfolio adds
  P&L across currencies as one number. The database now holds both rouble and dollar bonds; a
  portfolio mixing them is not currently meaningful. All examples stay in roubles.
* **Only listed bonds can be ingested.** Both Finam calendar endpoints return nothing for an
  archived issue, so a bond universe from this connector has survivorship bias by construction.
  A live bond's *history* is complete.
* **Floating-rate bonds are incomplete.** Coupons whose rate the vendor has not fixed are ingested
  as zero-value events and pay nothing. Fixed-coupon issues are exact.
* **Settlement type is not machine-verified.** Finam exposes no such field, so the CASH/PHYSICAL
  classification is domain knowledge and should be reviewed against contract specs. It moves
  results — see §2.
* **Margin is modelled, not enforced.** No margin calls, no buying-power constraint.
* **Delivery is avoided, not simulated.** A deliverable position is force-closed before its notice
  date; assignment and squeeze risk are not modelled.
* **`data.current` is not positional.** It takes a *set* of assets and omits any with no bar today,
  so reading its rows by position can pair a price with the wrong instrument. Every example keys on
  `sid`; nothing in the API enforces it.
* **MOEX fee tables are placeholders.** Order-of-magnitude only; replace with your broker's
  schedule.
* **Root symbols are namespaced, not composite-keyed.** Non-MOEX roots are `CODE.MIC` because
  `futures_root_symbols` keys a chain by root symbol alone. A composite `(root_symbol, mic)` key is
  the modelling-correct fix and needs a wider migration — flagged for review in
  [`changes.md`](changes.md) §3.3.
* **Connector dependencies are mandatory.** `yfinance` and `h2` are used by one connector each but
  are non-optional in `pyproject.toml`. Making them extras changes what `poetry install` produces,
  so it was left as a maintainer decision.
* **Price limits, locked markets and stale-price `can_trade()`** are disclosed, not modelled.

`context.realism_warnings()` reports the runtime-relevant subset of this list during a simulation,
so a result is read with its gaps attached rather than having them rediscovered later.
